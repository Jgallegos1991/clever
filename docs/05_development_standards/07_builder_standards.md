### 07_Builder_Standards.md

#### 1. Purpose: Realization over Invention
The central rule for all development is: **Builders may realize the architecture, but they may not redefine it** [3, 4]. This document defines the boundaries of authority for human developers and AI agents, ensuring that implementation remains a high-fidelity expression of the frozen blueprint [5, 6].

#### 2. Builder Authority and Limits
To preserve **Digital Sovereignty**, authority over the codebase is strictly tiered [4, 7, 8]:
*   **The Human Architect:** Holds ultimate authority over all levels of the architecture. Only the human partner or authorized human developers may propose or approve changes to the **Sovereign Runtime** or **Constitutional Foundations** (Levels 1–3) [9, 10].
*   **AI Builders:** AI agents are classified as **"Builders"** [11, 12]. AI Builders may implement, refactor, and modify software realization—including runtime implementations—provided those changes remain within the frozen architectural contracts. They may not independently redefine, amend, or bypass the architectural or constitutional foundations.

#### 3. Constitutional Boundaries (Being vs. Tools)
Every Builder must respect the distinction between Clever’s **Being** (Identity) and her **Tools** (Implementation) [13, 14]. 
*   **Downward Evolution:** Changes should happen downward through implementation rather than upward into architecture [15].
*   **Non-Intervention:** Implementation choices must never reverse the authority chain: **Philosophy → Logic → Engineering → Software → Implementation** [2, 3, 16].

#### 4. Required Pre-Change Checks
Before modifying any component, a Builder must ensure:
*   **Responsibility Audit:** Identify exactly what architectural responsibility the change serves [5, 17, 18].
*   **Agent Onboarding:** AI agents must demonstrate alignment with the **Sovereign Neural Framework** and the current architectural freeze [11, 12].
*   **Canonical Check:** Confirm that no other subsystem already handles the target responsibility to prevent **proliferation over canonicalization** [2, 19, 20].

#### 5. No Autonomous Architectural Changes
Architecture precedes engineering, and engineering precedes software [3, 16].
*   **Frozen Invariants:** Builders are prohibited from autonomously altering frozen Level 1–4 documents to resolve implementation-level constraints [3, 21].
*   **Structural Integrity:** Builders must prioritize structural fixes to the underlying implementation over temporary, symptomatic patches [15].

#### 6. Repository Stewardship as an Engineering Standard
A clean repository is a physical manifestation of the architecture [22].
*   **Minimalism:** Every new component or abstraction must justify its existence by providing a unique architectural responsibility [5, 20].
*   **One Source of Truth:** Duplicate definitions are forbidden; each concept has exactly one canonical home [20, 23].

#### 7. Verification and Traceability
Builders are accountable for the integrity of their modifications:
*   **Architectural Traceability:** Every change must be traceable back to a specific cognitive function or engineering requirement [24, 25].
*   **Verification:** All changes must pass the verification protocols defined in **05_Testing_Standards** and **06_Observability_Standards** to ensure they do not introduce side effects or architectural drift.

#### 8. Amendment Escalation
If a Builder identifies a fundamental flaw in the architecture:
*   **Agency Limit:** The Builder lacks the constitutional agency to implement the change [10].
*   **Escalation Protocol:** The Builder must raise an **Amendment Escalation** to the Human Architect [26].
*   **Burden of Proof:** The proposal must demonstrate that the change is necessitated by a flaw in foundational logic, not implementation convenience [7, 25].
